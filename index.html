Ref. 03 — Who this is for

Residential landlords

Freeholders and building owners

Purpose-built blocks of flats and converted houses. If you own or manage the common parts, the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 puts the legal duty on you — whether you self-manage or use an agent.

HMO landlords

Houses in multiple occupation

Shared housing carries its own risk profile and, in many boroughs, licensing requirements that expect a current fire risk assessment. Gets checked at renewal — worth having it right before then, not after.

Managing agents

Acting on behalf of others

If you manage blocks or HMOs for landlords who may not know an assessment is overdue or was never done, a clear report gives you something concrete to act on — and to show them.

Small business owners

Shops, offices, and similar premises

Since October 2023, a written fire risk assessment is a legal requirement regardless of how many people you employ. A one-person shop is treated the same as a large office under the law.

Ref. 04 — Why it matters

One building-height figure changes what you're legally required to do.

The height of a building triggers a cascade of separate duties — sprinkler requirements, signage, secure information boxes, evacuation alert systems, and in some cases full Higher-Risk Building status. A proper assessment traces that cascade through, rather than working from a generic checklist.

Common gap

A laminated Fire Action Notice in the lobby does not, on its own, satisfy Regulation 6 of the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022. Active, individual resident information is a separate legal duty.
