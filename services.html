Services

What's covered

Non-intrusive fire risk assessments for residential and low-risk commercial buildings. Every assessment is written up in plain terms, with actions that state the fix — not just the fault.

Type 1

Residential — Type 1 fire risk assessment

Non-intrusive assessment of common parts in purpose-built blocks of flats and converted houses, to BS 9792:2025. Covers the standard scope landlords and managing agents need under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 and the Fire Safety Act 2021.

  • Fire doors, self-closing devices, and compartmentation in common areas
  • Escape routes, signage, and emergency lighting adequacy
  • Evacuation strategy check — stay-put vs simultaneous, matched against what's actually installed
  • Alarm system grading and maintenance regime review
  • Resident information duties under Regulation 6
Type 3

Residential — Type 3 fire risk assessment

A more detailed non-intrusive assessment, typically used where a Type 1 has flagged concerns that need closer examination, or where the building's history or complexity calls for it from the outset.

  • Wider sampling across common parts and accessible documentation
  • Closer inspection of fire doors — degraded intumescent strips, non-compliant closers, glazing integrity
  • Building height threshold review and its knock-on compliance effects
Commercial — low risk

Commercial fire risk assessment

Non-intrusive assessment of low-risk commercial premises — offices, shops, and similar Purpose Group buildings — aligned to PAS 79-1:2020. At this stage, scope is limited to low-risk premises; more complex commercial buildings are outside current coverage.

  • Escape routes and final exits, including checks for locked or obstructed exits
  • Fixed installations — alarm, emergency lighting, extinguisher provision
  • Fire safety management arrangements and record-keeping
Approach

How reports are written

Reports separate compliant items ("Controls") from genuine deficiencies requiring action ("Actions"). Every action states the fix, not just the fault — so there's no guesswork about what needs to happen next. Findings are cross-referenced against current legislation and the relevant design standard for the building type, rather than a single generic checklist.
