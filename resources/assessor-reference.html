Assessor reference

Occupancy, exits and travel distance

Figures and links for use on site. Not linked from the public site. Last checked October 2026 from my notes. Check any figure in the current guide before relying on it, and say in the report which source you used.

Method

Occupancy is the lower of the floor space figure and the exit figure. Measure the public floor net of stairs, lifts, toilets, servery and fixed structure, and divide by the factor. For exits, discount the widest, convert each remaining width to people and add them up. Then check travel distances.

Mixed use: apply the most onerous factor to each part. Where people would normally be seated, the number of fixed seats can be used. If the use is not listed, choose a reasonable similar use. In un-seated areas, foyers or bars the guides allow 0.3 to 0.5 m² per person.

Exit capacity

HM Government fire safety risk assessment guides. People allowed through a given clear width.

Each extra 75 mm (or part of)+15+15+15
  • Discount the widest exit, as it may be blocked by the fire. If two exits are close together, consider whether one fire could stop both.
  • The guides give only the 750 mm and 1050 mm figures. For a width in between, use the lower figure.
  • Doors should open in the direction of escape where more than 60 people are expected. Count an inward-opening exit as 60 unless trained staff hold it open as part of fire safety management.
  • Residential care and healthcare have no persons-per-width table, and open air venues use flow rates. See the guide.
  • Approved Document B Table 2.3 gives different figures (110 for 850 mm, 220 for 1050 mm). Say which source you used.

Healthcare30 m (dependent patients), 45 m (independent)15 m (dependent), 18 m (independent)
  • Measure from any point to the nearest place of reasonable safety: a protected stairway, a separate fire compartment with a final exit, or a final exit.
  • Normal risk is the usual starting point. Higher risk is for small higher-risk areas. Lower risk is for exceptional cases only.
  • If the first part of the route is one direction only, apply the single route figure to that part. The total must not exceed the more-than-one-route figure.
  • Healthcare: total travel within a compartment should not exceed 60 m. In sleeping accommodation the distance inside the bedroom counts toward the total.
